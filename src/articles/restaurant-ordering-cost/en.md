Paying less commission does not always mean you keep more money. The better question is: **after paying the app, payment provider, rider and staff, how much do you have left from the same order?**

Delivery apps can help people discover your restaurant. Your own ordering website may suit regular customers who already know you. You can use both. Let’s compare the costs before deciding.

## Let’s say a customer orders RM50 of food
**What does “basket” mean?** It is the total price of the food and drinks in one order, before delivery fees, service charges and any other checkout charges. In this guide, we call it the **order amount**. For example, two meals at RM20 each and two drinks at RM5 each make a RM50 basket.

This is a **made-up example to show the maths**, not an actual GrabFood or foodpanda fee or a promise of savings. The customer orders the same RM50 meal. We leave out food and packaging costs because they are the same on both sides.

| What we are counting | Example cost |
|---|---|
| App charges paid by the restaurant, including tax | 20% of order value |
| Website setup, divided over 24 months | RM1,688 ÷ 24 |
| Website subscription | RM168/month |
| Website payment fee, assuming 8% tax on the fee | RM1.25 × 1.08 = RM1.35/order |
| Delivery cost paid by the restaurant after the customer’s share | RM3/order |
| Extra staff time and getting customers to order on the website | RM1.50/order |

The app takes RM10 per order in this example. Through the website, payment, delivery and extra staff/customer costs add up to RM5.85. That leaves a difference of **RM4.15 per order**. The website plan plus a monthly share of setup comes to **RM238.33/month**. We assume no other monthly fees here; add yours if there are any.

| Similar orders/month | Platform charges | Website costs, with setup spread over 24 months | How much less the website costs |
|---|---|---|---|

**With these numbers, the website starts costing less at about 58 similar orders a month.** Below that, the app costs less in this example. This is a cost comparison, not your restaurant’s profit. It only works if those customers actually order through your website and the costs stay as shown.

## How much do GrabFood and foodpanda charge restaurants?
Your rate may be different from the restaurant next door. [Grab’s merchant page](https://www.grab.com/my/merchant/food/) confirms service fees and describes its ordering and delivery offering. [Foodpanda’s registration FAQ](https://partner.foodpanda.my/en) says the chosen commission package determines the exact rate shown during registration.

Check your agreement and recent payment statements. Count the commission, tax, paid ads, discounts you fund and any monthly fee. Don’t count delivery paid by the customer as your own expense. Our 20% is just an example—you can change it.

Apps also help customers find you and arrange riders. A website does not do all that by itself. If customers stop ordering instead of moving to your website, you have not saved money on those lost sales.

## Why a small payment fee can take a bigger bite from a small order
At the time of this review, [Billplz’s pricing page](https://main.billplz.com/pricing) lists Basic-plan consumer FPX at RM1.25 per payment and wallet payments at 1.5%. These are different methods, not fees to automatically add together. Billplz’s [pricing guide](https://support.billplz.com/guide/pricing) explains that 8% SST applies to its transaction fees. Activation, plan and method conditions still need checking before choosing a provider.

| Order amount | RM1.25 fixed fee, before tax | RM1.35 fee, with assumed 8% fee tax |
|---|---|---|
| RM30 | 4.17% | 4.50% |
| RM50 | 2.50% | 2.70% |
| RM150 | 0.83% | 0.90% |

To find the payment fee as a percentage, **divide the total payment fee by the order amount, then multiply by 100**. For example: RM1.35 ÷ RM30 × 100 = 4.5%.

Use the fee for the payment method the customer actually uses. Don’t automatically add an FPX fee to a card or wallet fee. The calculator adds the tax rate you enter to the payment fee only. Check your provider’s tax rules if they differ. A cheaper rate may need a paid plan, so count that plan too.

## How many orders do you need before the website costs less?
Start with two numbers:

- **Your website's monthly cost:** the monthly plan, other monthly fees and a share of the setup price.
- **What you save on each order:** the app's charges minus what you pay to handle the same order through your website.

Then use: **extra monthly website cost ÷ saving per order = orders needed**. Round up to the next full order. If the app also charges a monthly fee, subtract it from the website's monthly cost first.

In our example: **RM238.33 ÷ RM4.15 = about 58 orders a month**.

If each website order costs more to handle, getting more orders will not automatically help. Sometimes the app is cheaper. Sometimes the website has lower monthly costs but higher costs per order, so it only costs less at a smaller order volume. The calculator checks these situations too.

**Cheaper each month does not mean you get your setup money back straight away.** Your quote sets the payment schedule. If you pay all RM1,688 upfront, the example at 100 orders/month leaves RM247/month towards that setup cost after the RM168 plan fee. That takes about 7 months, assuming no other extra costs.

Your first month’s cash payment would be RM2,441, not RM823.33. The table spreads setup across 24 months to help compare costs; it does not show your actual monthly bill.

## Compare pickup with pickup, delivery with delivery
- Compare pickup with pickup, and delivery with delivery. A pickup website order has no rider cost; that does not make it equivalent to a platform-delivered order.
- For third-party riders, obtain current quotes for the restaurant’s location, distance, hours and service requirements. Coverage, availability and charges vary. No provider connection or delivery speed is assumed here.
- Count only the delivery cost borne by the restaurant after taking off the amount paid by the customer, plus redelivery, cancellations and refunds where applicable.
- Include extra staff time for order acceptance, dispatch, customer questions and checking payments. Ask who handles a failed payment or missed rider collection.
- Check POS menu, sales and refund support before buying. API access and provider approval are not universal, and custom integration can add setup and ongoing costs.

## When you may not need an ordering website yet
You may not need an ordering website yet if you have few regular customers, struggle to manage orders or need costly system connections for only a few orders. A clear menu, accurate opening hours and a straightforward enquiry or reservation request form may address the immediate problem first.

Start small: keep the apps that bring customers, make website ordering easy for regulars who want it, and check the results after a trial. Do not treat a customer email list or a new website as proof that orders will move automatically.

## Common questions
### Is a direct ordering website always cheaper?
No. It depends on similar order volume, fixed costs, payment fees, delivery and staff time and the cost of getting customers. A lower headline commission is not enough.

### Should I stop using delivery apps?
Not on the basis of this calculation alone. Discovery, convenience and customer preference matter. Look at the costs, sales and what your customers actually use before making changes.

### Does this calculator include all taxes and refunds?
No. You can change the tax on payment fees and the app charges in the calculator. Use the figures in your agreement and add other costs. It does not work out your business taxes, refunds or actual profit.

### Will the calculator store my sales figures?
No. This calculator runs in the browser, does not save its inputs and does not submit them. Avoid entering private account or customer information; it only needs cost assumptions.
